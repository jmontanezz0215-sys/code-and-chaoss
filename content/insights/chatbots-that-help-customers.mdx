# A chatbot should help, not get in the way

## Define a small, useful job

A website assistant can answer common service questions, explain package scope, and help visitors find the right form. It does not need to pretend it can handle every possible request. A clear role makes both its responses and its limitations easier to understand.

## Start with approved business information

Keep services, pricing, contact details, and frequently asked questions in an editable knowledge base. Unverified information should stay out of the assistant’s answers. When a fact is unknown, the assistant should say so and offer a useful next step.

## Make the handoff obvious

Visitors should always have a way to contact a person. A chatbot can direct them to a call link or inquiry form without claiming that a booking happened. Separate answering a question from saving a request or confirming an appointment.

## Ask only for what you need

If the visitor wants follow-up, collect the information needed for that purpose and explain how it will be used. Avoid requesting sensitive information in chat. A clear form often provides a better place for contact details.

## Keep a fallback available

External AI services can be unavailable. A focused FAQ fallback and reliable contact route keep the website useful. Treat the assistant as support for your team, not the only path to help.
